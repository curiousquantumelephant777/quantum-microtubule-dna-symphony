## Abstract

This notebook-style repository synthesizes ideas on how piezoelectric microtubule lattices, vibrational quanta (phonons), and nuclear chromatin interact to influence gene expression, solute transport, and conscious experience. Core motifs include aromatic ring coherence and delocalization effects, LINC-mediated force transmission reframed as quantized phonons, and string theory-inspired DNA CpG island epigenetic modification. The LINC complex serves as a mechanical bridge where cytosolic force is converted into chromatin reorganization via mechanotransduction pathways.

Non-coding and Z-DNA regions could act as high-gain resonators, and CpG islands are the "tuning switches." In the absence of sufficient GDNF-mediated synaptogenesis (e.g., in ASD), this leads to an overwhelming high-intensity electroneurochemical signal that disrupts the integration of social-reward stimuli.

## ⚖️ Falsifiablity and Disclaimer

This is a highly speculative concept, and is falsifiable by the idea that quantum coherence is very difficult to maintain in a "warm, wet, noisy" environment like the brain. This research posits that the brain and systemic cytoskeletal network is made up of millions of synchronized quantum processes that occur at nanoscales across massive lattices (similar to quantum tunneling in enzymes) that produce phononic phenomena and biophotonic emissions leading to Brilloun scattering eigenstates, which may bypass this. This research is purely theoretical and computational at this stage.
